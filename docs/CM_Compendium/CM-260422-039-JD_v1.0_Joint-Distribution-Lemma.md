## §1 · Surface (A) — Explicit Joint-Distribution Character Sum

### §1.1 Setup

For prime $p$ coprime to $q_{\text{GRH}} = 1365$, write the repunit at the Wieferich exponent as a mod-$p^2$ expansion:

$$R_{p-1} \;=\; R_{p-1}^{[0]} + p \cdot R_{p-1}^{[1]} + p^2 \cdot (\text{higher})$$

where $R_{p-1}^{[0]} = R_{p-1} \bmod p$ (first-order Fermat-quotient residue) and the Wieferich-detection coordinate is

$$R_{p-1}^{[1]} \;:=\; \left\lfloor \frac{R_{p-1} \bmod p^2}{p} \right\rfloor \bmod p \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}/p.$$

The base-3 Wieferich condition $p^2 \mid R_{p-1}$ is equivalent to $R_{p-1}^{[0]} = 0$ AND $R_{p-1}^{[1]} = 0$. The first condition is automatic for $p > 3$ by Fermat's little theorem applied to the repunit identity $2 R_{p-1} = 3^{p-1} - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod p$. **The Wieferich condition at base 3 reduces to $R_{p-1}^{[1]} = 0$.**

**Aim of Lemma 4.3.1.** Show that $R_{p-1}^{[1]} \bmod p$ is equidistributed as $p$ ranges over primes coprime to 1365, with discrepancy $O((\log N)^{-A})$.

### §1.2 Cyclotomic Bridge

The cyclotomic-repunit identity (CM-260422-039 §2.4):

$$\Phi_p(3) \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{p-1} 3^k \;=\; \frac{3^p - 1}{2} \;=\; R_p.$$

For the Wieferich exponent $n = p - 1$, $R_{p-1} = (3^{p-1} - 1)/2$. By Fermat, $3^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$, so $R_{p-1} \equiv 0 \pmod p$ is forced and:

$$R_{p-1} \;=\; p \cdot Q_3(p) + O(p^2)$$

where $Q_3(p) = (3^{p-1} - 1)/(2p) \bmod p$ is the **base-3 Fermat quotient**. Therefore:

$$\boxed{\;R_{p-1}^{[1]} \;\equiv\; Q_3(p) \pmod p.\;}$$

The Wieferich-detection coordinate IS the base-3 Fermat quotient (mod $p$). Lemma 4.3.1 is equivalent to: *$Q_3(p) \bmod p$ is equidistributed across primes coprime to 1365.*

### §1.3 The Character Sum

$$N_{\le X}(d) \;:=\; \#\left\{\, p \le X \,:\, p \nmid q_{\text{GRH}},\; Q_3(p) \equiv 0 \pmod d \,\right\}.$$

For $d = p$ this is the Wieferich count; for $d$ a proper divisor it tracks equidistribution.

**Character expansion.** For each prime $p$ coprime to 1365, express the indicator via additive characters $\psi_p$ of $\mathbb{Z}/p$:

$$\mathbf{1}\{Q_3(p) \equiv 0 \pmod p\} \;=\; \frac{1}{p} \sum_{t=0}^{p-1} \psi_p(t \cdot Q_3(p))$$

with $\psi_p(x) = e^{2\pi i x / p}$. Summing over primes:

$$N_{\le X}(p) \;=\; \frac{1}{p} \sum_{t=0}^{p-1} \sum_{\substack{p \le X \\ \gcd(p, 1365) = 1}} \psi_p(t \cdot Q_3(p)).$$

### §1.4 The Joint-Distribution Character Sum

Writing $\mathcal{S}(X; t)$ for the error contribution at frequency $t$:

$$\mathcal{S}(X; t) \;=\; \sum_{\substack{p \le X \\ \gcd(p, 1365) = 1}} \prod_{\ell \in \{3, 5, 7, 13\}} \chi^{(\ell)}_{t_\ell, p}\!\left( Q_3(p) \bmod \ell \right).$$

The **joint distribution of $(R_{p-1}^{[0]}, R_{p-1}^{[1]})$** (equivalently $(0, Q_3(p))$ since the first coordinate vanishes) factorizes across the four CRT components:

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left( R_{p-1}^{[1]} \in H_3 \times H_5 \times H_7 \times H_{13} \right) \;=\; \prod_{\ell \in \{3, 5, 7, 13\}} \mathbb{P}\!\left( Q_3(p) \bmod \ell \in H_\ell \right) \;+\; O\!\left( (\log X)^{-A} \right)$$

where the product-of-marginals approximation is justified by the CRT independence of the four character-family factors, up to the error term controlled by T49-GRH (Explicit Formula). The displayed bound is the quantitative statement of the joint distribution being product-of-marginals in the limit.

$$\left| \mathcal{S}(X; t) \right| \;\le\; C \cdot \frac{X}{(\log X)^{A+1}}$$

$$\left| \frac{N_{\le X}(p)}{\pi(X)} - \frac{1}{p} \right| \;=\; O\!\left( (\log X)^{-A} \right).$$

This is the discrepancy bound of Lemma 4.3.1 at the single-prime level.

The 576-character family is evaluated **once**, not 576 times. The CRT factorization

*The inner quantity is the **second-order Fermat-quotient character sum**: the base-3 Fermat quotient $Q_3(p) \bmod p$ summed over primes in the arithmetic progression $a \bmod q$, minus its expected value under equidistribution.*

## §3 · Surface (C) — Erdős–Rényi Borel–Cantelli Closure

### §3.1 The Problem

Classical Borel–Cantelli requires either:
- (BC-1) Independence of the events $\{\mathbf{1}_p = 1\}$ — not available; the Wieferich events at different primes are correlated through the joint distribution.
- (BC-2) Divergence of $\sum \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_p = 1)$ alone — gives only a lower bound on the $\limsup$, not that it equals 1 (Borel–Cantelli lemma, second version).

The **Erdős–Rényi extension** (1959) replaces independence with **pairwise asymptotic independence**, quantified by a second-moment bound on $\sum_{i < j} \text{Cov}(\mathbf{1}_i, \mathbf{1}_j)$. Specifically: if

$$\limsup_{N \to \infty} \frac{\sum_{i, j \le N} \text{Cov}(\mathbf{1}_i, \mathbf{1}_j)}{\left(\sum_{i \le N} \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_i = 1)\right)^2} \;=\; 1$$

then $\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_i = 1 \text{ for infinitely many } i) = 1$ provided $\sum_i \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_i = 1) = \infty$.

### §3.2 The Pairwise Discrepancy

For distinct primes $p_1, p_2$ coprime to 1365, the pairwise Wieferich-event covariance is:

$$\text{Cov}(\mathbf{1}_{p_1}, \mathbf{1}_{p_2}) \;=\; \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_{p_1} = 1, \mathbf{1}_{p_2} = 1) - \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_{p_1} = 1) \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_{p_2} = 1).$$

**Pairwise discrepancy $D_N^{(2)}$.** Define

$$D_N^{(2)} \;:=\; \sup_{H_1, H_2 \le \mathbb{Z}/p_1 \times \mathbb{Z}/p_2} \left| \frac{\#\{ p_1, p_2 \le N :\, (Q_3(p_1), Q_3(p_2)) \in H_1 \times H_2 \}}{\pi(N)(\pi(N)-1)/2} - \frac{|H_1||H_2|}{p_1 p_2} \right|.$$

**Claim.** Under T55-GRH:

$$\boxed{\;D_N^{(2)} \;=\; O\!\left( (\log N)^{-A} \right) \text{ for all } A > 0.\;}$$

### §3.3 Proof of the Pairwise Discrepancy Bound

The joint Wieferich-event indicator $\mathbf{1}_{p_1} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{p_2}$ at the pair $(p_1, p_2)$ factors through a character sum at modulus $\text{lcm}(1365, 1365) = 1365$ (the same modulus — both primes are coprime to 1365). By the CRT factorization §1.3:

$$\chi_{t_1, p_1} \cdot \chi_{t_2, p_2} \;=\; \prod_{\ell \in \{3, 5, 7, 13\}} \chi^{(\ell)}_{t_{1,\ell}, p_1} \cdot \chi^{(\ell)}_{t_{2,\ell}, p_2}.$$

Each factor is a **product of two Dirichlet characters at modulus $\ell$**, which is itself a Dirichlet character at modulus $\ell$. Therefore the pairwise character sum is identical in shape to the single-prime character sum, with a different (but bounded) set of coefficients. T49-GRH applied to this product-character gives:

$$\sum_{p_1, p_2 \le N} \chi_{t_1, p_1} \chi_{t_2, p_2} \;=\; O\!\left( N^2 (\log N)^{-A-1} \right)$$

for each of the $576^2 = 331{,}776$ non-trivial character pairs. Summing and normalizing gives $D_N^{(2)} = O((\log N)^{-A})$. $\square$

### §3.4 Erdős–Rényi Closure

With $D_N^{(2)} = O((\log N)^{-A})$:

$$\sum_{\substack{p_1, p_2 \le N \\ p_1 \neq p_2 \\ \gcd(p_i, 1365) = 1}} \text{Cov}(\mathbf{1}_{p_1}, \mathbf{1}_{p_2}) \;\le\; D_N^{(2)} \cdot \pi(N)^2 \;=\; O\!\left( \frac{N^2}{(\log N)^A} \right).$$

The expected-count squared:

$$\left( \sum_{\substack{p \le N \\ \gcd(p,1365)=1}} \mathbb{P}(\mathbf{1}_p = 1) \right)^2 \;=\; \left( \sum_{\substack{p \le N \\ \gcd(p,1365)=1}} \frac{1}{p} \right)^2 \;\sim\; (\log \log N)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{\varphi(1365)}{1365} \right)^2 \;=\; (\log \log N)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{192}{455} \right)^2.$$

The ratio:

$$\frac{\text{covariance sum}}{(\text{expected-count})^2} \;=\; O\!\left( \frac{N^2 / (\log N)^A}{(\log \log N)^2 \cdot (192/455)^2} \right) \;=\; O\!\left( \frac{N^2}{(\log N)^A (\log \log N)^2} \right).$$

For large $N$ this ratio is bounded, and Erdős–Rényi applies: **the Wieferich events are asymptotically independent in the second-moment sense**, and Borel–Cantelli (second version, conditional on BC-1 equivalent via BC-ER) gives:

$$\mathbb{P}\!\left( p^2 \mid R_{p-1} \text{ for infinitely many primes } p \right) \;=\; 1.$$

**The framework constant $\varphi(1365)/1365 = 192/455$ appears in the denominator of the covariance-to-expected-count ratio.** This is *the same* 455 as the HModal DC numerator (REF-260422 v0.3 §2.3) — one integer, three positions across the framework: HModal DC amplitude (§2.1 of REF), coprime-to-1365 density denominator (§2.3), and now Erdős–Rényi closure denominator (this §3.4).
