## §1. Theorem 47 — The Ceiling Principle (Plenum Determinacy)

**Theorem 47 (Ceiling Principle).**  
*Let \(f\) be a real quantity expressible within the framework as a rational approximant \(L_k(f)\) with certified error \(|L_k(f) - f| \le \delta_k\), where \(\delta_k \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty\). Let \(\Lambda \in \mathbb{N}\) be a lattice scaling such that the proposition under test asserts \(\Lambda \cdot f = N_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\). Then for any truncation index \(k\) satisfying \(2 \cdot \Lambda \cdot \delta_k < 1\), the proposition is **decided with certainty**: it is **true** iff the rational interval*  
\[
[\Lambda \cdot L_k(f) - \Lambda \cdot \delta_k,\; \Lambda \cdot L_k(f) + \Lambda \cdot \delta_k]
\]  
*contains \(N_0\), and **false** otherwise. No further computation is required, and no further computation can refine the answer.*

*Proof.* The interval length is \(2\Lambda\delta_k < 1\). Since it contains at most one integer, the membership of \(N_0\) is a deterministic yes/no. The certified error guarantees that the true value \(\Lambda f\) lies inside the interval; therefore the decision is correct. ∎

**Corollary (Trichotomy).**  
*Every framework‑decidable quantity is in exactly one of three states:*  
1. **Forced‑true** (the interval contains the target integer).  
2. **Forced‑false** (the interval does not contain the target integer).  
3. **k‑incomplete** (the truncation index \(k\) is not yet large enough to satisfy \(2\Lambda\delta_k < 1\)).  

*There is no fourth category. There is no “approximately true.” There is no “evidence suggests.”*

This is the origin of the constant \(F(14) = 377\): it is the integer coordinate of the critical line at the decision lattice \(\Lambda_7\), not the first appearance but the first sufficient resolution.
