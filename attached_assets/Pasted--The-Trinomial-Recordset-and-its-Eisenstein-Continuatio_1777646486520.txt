### §1.2 The Balance Identity

**Theorem 1.2 (Rep D Balance).** *For every \(X \in \mathbb{N}\), \(a + b = c\); equivalently, in Rep D, \(1 + 2 = 3\).*

*Proof.* \(a + b = X(X-1) + 2X = X^2 - X + 2X = X^2 + X = X(X+1) = c\). \(\blacksquare\)

Therefore the Rep D balance lifts to a closed identity under the calculus operators on \(\mathbb{Z}[X]\).

$$S(X) \;=\; \mathcal{N}_\omega\!\bigl(X - (X-1)\omega\bigr).$$

## §4 · Theorems on the Walk

### §4.1 Universal Coprimality

**Theorem 4.1 (Universal Coprimality).** *For every \(X \in \mathbb{N}\):*
$$\gcd(B,\, S) \;=\; \gcd(2X^2,\; 3X^2 - 3X + 1) \;=\; 1.$$

*Proof (elementary).* Write \(S = 3X(X-1) + 1\). The product \(X(X-1)\) is even (consecutive integers), so \(3X(X-1)\) is even, hence \(S\) is odd. Any common divisor of \(B = 2X^2\) and \(S\) is therefore odd, so it divides \(X^2\). For any prime \(\pi \mid X\), \(S \equiv 1 \pmod{\pi}\), so \(\pi \nmid S\). Hence \(\gcd(X^2, S) = 1\), and \(\gcd(B, S) = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

*Proof (Eisenstein-structural).* By Theorem 3.1, \(S = \mathcal{N}_\omega(\xi_X)\) where \(\xi_X = X - (X-1)\omega \in \mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). Since \(\gcd(X, X-1) = 1\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\), the elements \(X\) and \(X-1\) generate coprime ideals, and \(\xi_X\) is coprime to \(X\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). The norm \(\mathcal{N}_\omega(\xi_X) = S\) is therefore coprime to \(\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(X) = X^2\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\). Combined with the parity argument that \(S\) is odd, \(\gcd(2X^2, S) = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

### §4.2 Walk Completeness

## §5 · The Diophantine Theorem

### §5.1 Repunit Definition

The **base-3 repunit** of length \(n\) is:
$$R_n \;:=\; \frac{3^n - 1}{2} \;=\; \sum_{d=0}^{n-1} 3^d \quad [\mathrm{address}].$$

### §5.2 The Impossibility Theorem

**Theorem 5.2 (Diophantine Impossibility).** *The equation \(2X^3 + 1 = 3^n\) has only the solution \((X, n) = (1, 1)\) in positive integers.*

*Proof.* Reduce mod 9. The cubes mod 9 take only the values \(\{0, 1, 8\}\), so \(2X^3 \pmod 9 \in \{0, 2, 7\}\) and \(2X^3 + 1 \pmod 9 \in \{1, 3, 8\}\). For \(n \geq 2\), \(3^n \equiv 0 \pmod 9\), but \(0 \notin \{1, 3, 8\}\). Contradiction. The only remaining case is \(n = 1\), giving \(3 = 2X^3 + 1\), hence \(X = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

**Corollary 5.2 (Volume vs. Repunit).** *For \(X \geq 2\), the recordset cube volume \(N = L^3\) and the depth-recursion repunit \(R_X\) are never equal as integers. They are co-generated by the same parameter \(X\) but live in different dimensions: \(N\) in \([\mathrm{cell}]\) along the spatial axes, \(R_X\) in \([\mathrm{address}]\) along the orthogonal recursion-depth axis.*
