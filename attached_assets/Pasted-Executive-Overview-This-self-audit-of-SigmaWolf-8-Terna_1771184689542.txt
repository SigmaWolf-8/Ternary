Executive Overview
This self-audit of SigmaWolf-8/Ternary (GitHub) and PlenumNET (Replit) identifies medium-risk exposure primarily from international-law, export-control, licensing, and supply-chain considerations, with additional posture risks tied to security practices, data privacy, and potential financial/crypto activities. The work-scope centers on licensing attribution, dependency management, data flows, security hygiene, and platform hosting terms. The Veritas audit notes certain claims as disputed or unverified, particularly around regional legal fragmentation and cross-border harmonization; accordingly, where applicable, conclusions are qualified and recommendations emphasize due diligence and governance before broad distribution. A formal compliance program is warranted to inventory licenses, maintain SBOMs, ensure notices, screen for export controls, and obtain external legal review prior to wider release.

Key Findings by Domain
- Public International Law
  - Applicable: Yes; risk level: medium.
  - Core concerns include licensing and attribution, dependency and supply-chain risk, data privacy and cross-border data flows (especially for PlenumNET on Replit), security and vulnerability disclosure, cryptography/export controls, IP attribution, terms of use/governance, and platform hosting terms.
  - International-law anchors referenced by specialist inputs point to TRIPS protection of software as a literary work, and to export-control regimes (e.g., Wassenaar) and sanctions regimes that shape cross-border distribution and end-use restrictions.

- Security
  - Initial assessment identifies medium risk contingent on deeper code/dependency analysis. Unknowns in code quality and security practices imply potential supply-chain weaknesses and gaps in secure development, inventorying components, and verification against secure coding standards. Actionable steps depend on access to codebase and assets to perform SBOM generation, vulnerability scanning, and configuration hygiene.

- Data Privacy and Cross-Border Flows
  - The PlenumNET Replit app implicates data protection regimes (GDPR/UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, etc.). Requirements include mapping data flows, identifying personal data processing, and evaluating safeguards for transfers to third-country recipients (e.g., US-hosting on Replit) with appropriate DPAs and Standard Contractual Clauses.

- Licensing, Dependency, and Attribution
  - Critical to identify licenses for repository and bundled dependencies, preserve license notices, and respect copyleft obligations if distributing or deploying software. Transitive licenses must be reviewed to avoid unintended constraints.

- Platform/Hosting Terms
  - Compliance with GitHub and Replit terms of service is essential, including terms governing hosting, distribution, and data handling. Clear governance around terms of use, data processing, and jurisdiction is required.

Risk Assessment
- International Law/Export Controls: Medium. Due diligence required; potential exposure from TRIPS-based copyright obligations, and Wassenaar/sanctions-related end-use constraints in cross-border distribution.
- Supply-Chain/Dependency Management: Medium. Unknowns in dependency hygiene and vulnerability exposure without a formal SBOM and ongoing scanning.
- Data Privacy: Medium. Potential GDPR/CCPA exposure if personal data is processed, with cross-border transfers needing SCCs or DPAs.
- Security Posture: Medium. Preliminary; contingent on code quality, secure SDLC adherence, and vulnerability management capabilities.
- Crypto/Financial Implications: Conditional. If codebase evolves to support payments, tokens, wallets, or on-chain interactions, additional AML/CFT, KYC/CDD, and securities considerations apply.

Jurisdictional Analysis
- The international-law framework anchors include copyright (TRIPS) and export-control regimes (Wassenaar) with sanctions considerations. The Veritas audit flags a disputed/regional-law element concerning Shariah governance and fragmentation in regional financial regulation, recommending caution and verification; this item is not presented as a universal fact and requires targeted regional review. Likewise, claims about cross-border harmonization of regional data protection/consumer regimes are unverified and should be treated as contingent on jurisdiction-specific analyses. Given the absence of a complete regional findings package, proceed with a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction risk map anchored in recognized international-law standards and in-country legal counsel input.

Recommendations
- Governance and Inventory
  - Establish a formal license inventory for all code, assets, and bundled dependencies; preserve licenses and notices; document copyleft implications where distributing.
  - Generate and maintain an up-to-date Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) for the project lineage and third-party components.
  - Implement ongoing dependency vulnerability scanning and a patch-management process; assign accountability for SBOM and vulnerability remediation.

- Data Privacy and Cross-Border Transfers
  - Map data processed by PlenumNET and assess whether personal data is involved. If so, implement data-maps, a privacy policy aligned with applicable regimes, a DPA with hosting providers, and mechanisms for cross-border transfers (SCCs or equivalent) as needed.
  - Consider data localization or regional hosting assessments if required by jurisdictions.

- Security and Incident Response
  - Apply secure SDLC practices; conduct static/dynamic analysis and component inventories; align with secure-coding standards.
  - Establish a vulnerability disclosure policy and a responsible disclosure channel; implement incident response and recovery planning.

- Cryptography and Export Controls
  - If cryptographic components are used or shipped, confirm export-control classification and any registration or licensing obligations; consult counsel as needed.

- Intellectual Property and Documentation
  - Validate authorship credits and any contributor agreements; ensure clear attribution for all assets (images, fonts, icons) to avoid IP infringement.

- Terms, Hosting, and Governance
  - Draft or refine terms of use and privacy policy; define governing law and dispute resolution; ensure GitHub/Replit hosting terms compliance and data-handling practices.

Conclusion
The self-audit identifies tangible international-law and governance risks that merit a structured, proactive compliance program before broader distribution. A targeted action plan—centered on licensing inventories, SBOM maintenance, data-privacy safeguards, security hardening, and export-control diligence—will reduce exposure and enable safer, policy-aligned deployment of SigmaWolf-8/Ternary and PlenumNET. Continuous monitoring and jurisdiction-specific reviews are essential given the disputed/unverified aspects flagged by Veritas and the evolving regulatory landscape.
