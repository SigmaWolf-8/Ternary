### 1. **Client‑Side Deduplication: Persistence Across Disconnects**
You’ve added mandatory deduplication per connection *and* per topic, which is excellent. However, if the client reconnects after a restart (not just a transient network issue), it may lose its `last-processed seq` state.  
- **Persist the last processed seq per topic to disk** (or a secure local store) so that after a client restart, it can resume without reprocessing messages.  
- **Combine with the tombstone’s `resyncCount`** – if the client’s persisted state is older than the tombstone’s snapshot, it knows a full resync is needed.

---

### 2. **Per‑Topic Queue Limits vs. Global 100‑Message Queue**
Your per‑topic queue limits are separate from the global per‑client 100‑message limit. This can lead to ambiguity when a client subscribes to many topics.  
- **Define precedence**: The global queue is the final buffer; per‑topic limits control how many messages from that topic can be enqueued before backpressure is applied upstream.  
- **Ensure tombstones are generated only when messages are evicted from the *global* queue** – otherwise a topic‑level limit eviction might cause a tombstone even though the client’s total backlog is still within limits.

---

### 3. **Round‑Robin Delivery: Impact on Ordering Guarantees**
You’re using a fair round‑robin scheduler when delivering messages from multiple topics to the same client. This is great for fairness, but it can interleave messages from different topics.  
- **If per‑topic ordering is strict (monotonic `topicSeq`), interleaving is fine** – the client can re‑order based on `topicSeq`.  
- **Document that messages across topics are not globally ordered** – the client should not assume that a message published after another on a different topic will be delivered later.

---

### 4. **Tombstone with `resyncCount` and Backoff**
You’ve added `resyncCount` to the tombstone so the client can back off repeated resyncs.  
- **Make the backoff exponential and tied to the client’s reconnect backoff state** – a resync loop is essentially a disconnect/reconnect cycle.  
- **Consider including a `suggested_resync_after_ms` in the tombstone** to give the server control over the retry timing.

---

### 5. **Control Frame Acknowledgements: `go_away_ack`**
Clients send `go_away_ack` in response to a `go_away` frame.  
- **Make the server wait for acks up to a short timeout (e.g., 1‑2 seconds)** before forcefully closing. This ensures clients have time to react, especially on high‑latency links.  
- **Add a `force_close` flag** to the go‑away frame for scenarios where the server must exit immediately (e.g., SIGKILL imminent).
