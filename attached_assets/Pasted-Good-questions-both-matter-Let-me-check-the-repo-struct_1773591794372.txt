## Performance Impact

The short answer: **negligible**. Here's why.

The current chi step computes x¹⁷ over GF(27), which requires multiple field multiplications in GF(3³) — that's the expensive part of each round. The proposed affine layer adds a 3×3 matrix-vector multiply over GF(3) plus a constant addition. That's 9 GF(3) multiplications (which are single-digit mod-3 ops), 9 GF(3) additions, and 3 more additions for the constant vector. We're talking about ~21 trivial integer operations per S-box invocation, on top of the power map that's already doing the heavy lifting.

To put it in context against your current benchmarks:

The existing TLSponge-385 throughput is ~10 MB/s with AVX2/NEON SIMD and bulk squeeze. The affine map is a linear operation — it vectorizes perfectly under SIMD. A 3×3 matrix multiply over {0,1,2} values fits entirely in registers. On AVX2, you could process multiple S-box evaluations in parallel within a single vector instruction. On NEON (your ARM64 path at ~3.2 µs per sponge hash), the same applies.

Rough estimate: the affine layer adds somewhere between 2–5% overhead per round. Across 24 rounds, you might see throughput drop from ~10 MB/s to ~9.5–9.8 MB/s. For TIS-27 (the smaller sponge at 191 ns/hash, 2.52 GB/s), the impact would be proportionally similar — a few percent at most.

The power map x¹⁷ will still dominate the round function cost. The affine layer is a rounding error by comparison.
