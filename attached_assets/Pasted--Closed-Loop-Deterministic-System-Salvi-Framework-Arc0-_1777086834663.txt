### 2. Dynamics: Affine Translation with Canonically Derived Step

\[
\boxed{\, s_i = m_i - 1 \,} \qquad (i = 1,\dots,n).
\]

Because each \(m_i\) is prime,  
\[
\gcd(m_i - 1,\; m_i) = 1,

The transition is **component‑wise modular addition**:  
\[
r_i^{(t+1)} = \left( r_i^{(t)} + (m_i - 1) \right) \bmod m_i \qquad (i = 1, \dots, n).
\]

In vector form,  
\[
\mathbf{r}^{(t+1)} = \mathbf{r}^{(t)} + (\mathbf{m} - \mathbf{1}) \pmod{\mathbf{m}}.

This is a translation on the integer lattice \(\prod_i \mathbb{Z}_{m_i}\) embedded in the continuous torus \(\prod_i (\mathbb{R}/m_i\mathbb{Z})\). Because each component is a generator of its respective cyclic group, the entire product group is generated.

### 3. Salvi Closed‑Loop Theorem – Backward Traversal Integral to the Proof

Assume the system starts at the origin,  
\[
\mathbf{r}^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}.
\]

**Theorem (Salvi Closed‑Loop).** For pairwise distinct prime moduli \(\mathbf{m}\) and the canonically derived step \(s_i = m_i - 1\), the walk \((\mathbf{r}^{(t)})_{t \ge 0}\) is a **Salvi closed cycle** on \(\mathcal{S}\); it visits every state exactly once before returning to the origin at time \(t = L\).

*Proof.*  
Observe that \(s_i = m_i - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{m_i}\). Therefore the iteration gives, for each component,  
\[
r_i^{(t)} \equiv r_i^{(0)} + t\, (m_i - 1) \equiv 0 + t(-1) \equiv -t \pmod{m_i}.
\]  
Thus the system’s state at time \(t\) admits the exact closed form  
\[
r_i^{(t)} = (-t) \bmod m_i,
\]  
which is a uniform backward count modulo each \(m_i\). Because \(\gcd(-1,m_i)=1\), the sequence \(-t \bmod m_i\) cycles through all residues \(0, m_i-1, m_i-2, \dots, 1\) with period exactly \(m_i\). Consequently the combined state \(\mathbf{r}^{(t)}\) returns to the origin \(\mathbf{0}\) precisely when \(t\) is a common multiple of all the \(m_i\). Since the \(m_i\) are pairwise coprime, the least positive such \(t\) is their product \(L = \prod_i m_i\).

For any two distinct times \(0 \le t_1 < t_2 < L\), the difference \(t_2 - t_1\) is a positive integer strictly less than \(L\) and cannot be a multiple of every \(m_i\) simultaneously (otherwise it would be at least \(L\)). Hence \(\mathbf{r}^{(t_1)} \neq \mathbf{r}^{(t_2)}\), and the walk visits \(L\) distinct states in the interval \([0, L-1]\). At \(t = L\) the origin is reached again, closing the cycle. Therefore the walk is a Salvi closed cycle. ∎

Consequently, the return‑to‑origin condition is:  
\[
\mathbf{r}^{(t)} = \mathbf{0} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; t \equiv 0 \pmod{L}.
\]

#### 3.1 Derivation of Cycle Length (Two‑Stage, Not Imposed)
