This compendium establishes its own definitions where standard mathematical terminology is too narrow to express the structural unity of the framework. $$\mathcal{N}_\omega(p + q\omega) := (p + q\omega)(p + q\omega^2) = p^2 - pq + q^2.$$

### §1.2 The Balance Identity

**Theorem 51 (Rep D Balance).** *For every \(X \in \mathbb{N}\), \(a + b = c\); equivalently, in Rep D, \(1 + 2 = 3\).*

*Proof.* \(a + b = X(X-1) + 2X = X^2 - X + 2X = X^2 + X = X(X+1) = c\). \(\blacksquare\)

$$\frac{da}{dX} + \frac{db}{dX} = (2X - 1) + 2 = 2X + 1 = \frac{dc}{dX},$$
$$\int a\, dX + \int b\, dX = \left(\frac{X^3}{3} - \frac{X^2}{2}\right) + X^2 = \frac{X^3}{3} + \frac{X^2}{2} = \int c\, dX.$$

Therefore the Rep D balance lifts to a closed identity under the calculus operators on \(\mathbb{Z}[X]\).

Of these, only the \(b\)-axis is a pure quadratic in \(X\) without further \(X \pm 1\) modulation; the \(a\)- and \(c\)-axes are absorbed into the curvature of the cube's volume tier through the symmetric expansion of §2.2.

**Definition 2.3.0a (Buffer-Core).** The buffer-core is the \(b\)-axis amplification:
$$\boxed{\;B \;:=\; b \cdot X \;=\; 2X \cdot X \;=\; 2X^2 \quad [\mathrm{cell/axis}].\;}$$

The buffer-core is the **second-order linear-band footprint** of the trinomial — the unique pure-quadratic-in-\(X\) axis-product the trinomial supplies, with no \((X-1)\) or \((X+1)\) curvature factor.

The stride is derived along a different, complementary axis: the **discrete-derivative axis** of the cube function \(X^3\). The volume tier \(N = L^3\) cubes the side; its discrete generator is the backward finite difference \(\nabla\):
$$\nabla(f)(X) \;:=\; f(X) - f(X - 1).$$

**Definition 2.3.0b (Stride).** The stride is the backward finite difference of the cube function:
$$\boxed{\;S \;:=\; \nabla(X^3) \;=\; X^3 - (X - 1)^3 \;=\; 3X^2 - 3X + 1 \quad [\mathrm{cell/axis}].\;}$$

Expansion of the cube difference yields the symmetric form
$$S \;=\; X^2 + X(X - 1) + (X - 1)^2 \;=\; X^2 + a + (X - 1)^2,$$
where the middle term is exactly the Rep D letter "1" (the part \(a\)) of §1.1. The stride is therefore the Eisenstein-norm-bearing form of §3.1 (Theorem 55: \(S = \mathcal{N}_\omega(X - (X-1)\omega)\)) **and** the discrete-derivative axis of the cube simultaneously — the same object read on two operator axes.

**Theorem 52 (Walk Foundation).** *The buffer-core \(B = b \cdot X\) and the stride \(S = \nabla(X^3)\) together specify the walk on the recordset, with both parameters derived from the trinomial foundation:*

- *\(B\) is the second-order pure-quadratic amplification of the \(b\)-axis (linear band \(b = 2X\) times the parameter \(X\)).*

The complementarity: \(B(X) = 2X^2\) contains only the term \(X^2\) (no \(X-1\) factor), while \(S(X) = X^2 + X(X-1) + (X-1)^2\) is symmetric in \(X\) and \(X-1\), carrying the full \(\nabla\)-curvature of the cube. \(\blacksquare\)

## §3 · The Eisenstein Continuation

*The trinomial parts \(\{a, b, c\}\) and their balance \(a + b = c\) are the foundation. The three theorems of this section establish that the Eisenstein structure — norm forms, splitting behavior, cyclotomic factor — is present at every \(X\) as a **continuation** of the trinomial foundation, not as an enclosing substrate from which the trinomial is read. The Eisenstein continuation becomes **inherent** (rather than merely descriptive) through the cyclotomic portal at \(X = 8\) (§6).*

### §3.1 The Stride Splits over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\)

**Theorem 55 (Stride as Eisenstein Norm).** *For every \(X \in \mathbb{N}\),*
$$S(X) \;=\; \mathcal{N}_\omega\!\bigl(X - (X-1)\omega\bigr).$$
*Equivalently, \(S\) splits over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) in the sense of Definition CD.*

*Proof.* Direct expansion using \(\mathcal{N}_\omega(p - q\omega) = p^2 + pq + q^2\) with \(p = X\), \(q = X - 1\):
$$\mathcal{N}_\omega(X - (X-1)\omega) = X^2 + X(X-1) + (X-1)^2 = X^2 + (X^2 - X) + (X^2 - 2X + 1) = 3X^2 - 3X + 1 = S(X). \quad\blacksquare$$

**Note on strict factorization.** The polynomial \(S = 3X^2 - 3X + 1\) does not factor into linear factors with coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\); its roots \(X = (2 + \omega)/3\) and \(X = (1 - \omega)/3\) lie in \(\mathbb{Q}(\omega) \setminus \mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). The discriminant \(\operatorname{disc}(S) = -3\) — the field discriminant of \(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\) — confirms the splitting field is \(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\). The CD splitting (norm representation) captures the structural content; the strict polynomial factorization fails on a denominator of 3.

### §3.2 The Cube Identity Factor Splits over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) (Both Senses)

**Theorem 56 (Cube Identity Factorization).** *In \(\mathbb{Z}[X]\),*
$$a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc \;=\; 2X^3(X+1)\bigl(X^2 - 4X + 7\bigr).$$
*The factor \(X^2 - 4X + 7\) splits over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) in both the standard and the CD senses:*
- *(Standard)* \(X^2 - 4X + 7 = (X - (3 + 2\omega))(X - (1 - 2\omega))\) *with both roots in* \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\).
- *(CD)* \(X^2 - 4X + 7 = \mathcal{N}_\omega((X - 3) - 2\omega)\).

*Proof.* The polynomial identity \(a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a+b+c)(a^2+b^2+c^2 - ab - bc - ca)\), evaluated at \((a, b, c) = (X(X-1), 2X, X(X+1))\), gives \(2X(X+1) \cdot X^2(X^2 - 4X + 7) = 2X^3(X+1)(X^2 - 4X + 7)\). For the standard factorization: \((3 + 2\omega) + (1 - 2\omega) = 4\) and \((3 + 2\omega)(1 - 2\omega) = 3 - 4(\omega + \omega^2) = 3 + 4 = 7\). For CD: \(\mathcal{N}_\omega((X-3) - 2\omega) = (X-3)^2 + 2(X-3) + 4 = X^2 - 4X + 7\). \(\blacksquare\)

This theorem reveals the full Eisenstein factorization
$$a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc \;=\; (a + b + c)(a + \omega b + \omega^2 c)(a + \omega^2 b + \omega c)$$
collapsing on substitution to a structure where the symmetric Eisenstein factor is \(X^2 - 4X + 7\), itself split over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\).

### §3.3 The Cyclotomic Singularity Splits over \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\) (Both Senses)

**Theorem 57 (Cyclotomic Factor).** *Let \(\Phi_3(k) = k^2 + k + 1\) be the third cyclotomic polynomial. Then:*
- *(Standard)* \(\Phi_3(k) = (k - \omega)(k - \omega^2)\) *with both roots in* \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\).
- *(CD)* \(\Phi_3(k) = \mathcal{N}_\omega(k - \omega) = k^2 - k(-1) + (-1)^2 = k^2 + k + 1\).

*Proof.* \(\omega + \omega^2 = -1\) and \(\omega \cdot \omega^2 = \omega^3 = 1\), hence \((k - \omega)(k - \omega^2) = k^2 + k + 1\). The CD form follows from \(\mathcal{N}_\omega(p - q\omega) = p^2 + pq + q^2\) with \(p = k\), \(q = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

## §4 · Theorems on the Walk

### §4.1 Multiplicative Independence of Phase Offset and Stride-Companion

**Theorem 58 (Coprimality of B and S).** *For every \(X \in \mathbb{N}\):*
$$\gcd(B,\, S) \;=\; \gcd(2X^2,\; 3X^2 - 3X + 1) \;=\; 1.$$

*Proof (elementary).* Write \(S = 3X(X-1) + 1\). The product \(X(X-1)\) is even (consecutive integers), so \(3X(X-1)\) is even, hence \(S\) is odd. Any common divisor of \(B = 2X^2\) and \(S\) is therefore odd, so it divides \(X^2\). For any prime \(\pi \mid X\), \(S \equiv 1 \pmod{\pi}\), so \(\pi \nmid S\). Hence \(\gcd(X^2, S) = 1\), and \(\gcd(B, S) = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

*Proof (Eisenstein-structural).* By Theorem 55, \(S = \mathcal{N}_\omega(\xi_X)\) where \(\xi_X = X - (X-1)\omega \in \mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). Since \(\gcd(X, X-1) = 1\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\), the elements \(X\) and \(X-1\) generate coprime ideals, and \(\xi_X\) is coprime to \(X\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\). The norm \(\mathcal{N}_\omega(\xi_X) = S\) is therefore coprime to \(\mathcal{N}_\mathbb{Z}(X) = X^2\) in \(\mathbb{Z}\). Combined with the parity argument that \(S\) is odd, \(\gcd(2X^2, S) = 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

This theorem establishes the **multiplicative independence** of the phase offset \(B\) and the stride-companion invariant \(S\). It is a structural identity about the foundation, not the visit-completeness driver of the walk; the latter is established separately in Theorem 59 below using the universal coprimality of the stride \(u\) with the cube cardinality \(N\) (Corollary 54).

### §4.2 Operational Definition of the Walk

The walk requires three definitional components: the cell-index space, the seed cell, and the step rule.

**Definition 4.2.1 (Cell-Index Space).** *The cell-index space of the recordset is the bijection*
$$\Lambda \,:\, \{0, 1, \ldots, L-1\}^3 \;\longrightarrow\; \{0, 1, \ldots, N-1\}, \qquad \Lambda(i, j, k) \;:=\; i + j L + k L^2,$$
*where \((i, j, k)\) are the three orthogonal axis-coordinates of a cell in the cube of side \(L = 2X(X+1)\) and \(N = L^3\). The map \(\Lambda\) is a bijection because every integer \(m \in [0, N)\) has a unique base-\(L\) digit expansion \(m = i + j L + k L^2\) with \(0 \leq i, j, k < L\).*

**Definition 4.2.2 (Walk Seed).** *The walk's seed cell is the linear address*
