## §1 · Classical Background

### §1.1 Fermat's Little Theorem and the Wieferich Condition

For any prime $p$ and integer $a$ with $\gcd(a, p) = 1$, Fermat's little theorem gives $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$. The natural question is whether this congruence lifts to $p^2$. Generically, it does not: $(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has order $p(p-1)$, and the order of $a$ modulo $p^2$ is typically $p(p-1)$, not $p-1$.

A prime $p$ is **Wieferich** (base 2) if the lift holds:

$$2^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}.$$

Equivalently, the **Fermat quotient** $q_2(p) = (2^{p-1} - 1)/p \bmod p$ vanishes.

**Epistemic status:** Definition. Exact. [UPIID §E]

### §1.2 Historical Context

Wieferich proved in 1909 that if the first case of Fermat's Last Theorem fails for exponent $p$ — that is, if integers $x, y, z$ exist with $\gcd(xyz, p) = 1$ and $x^p + y^p = z^p$ — then $p$ must satisfy $2^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}$. At that time no such primes were known. Meissner found $p = 1093$ in 1913. Beeger found $p = 3511$ in 1922. No third Wieferich prime has been found below $6.7 \times 10^{15}$ despite extensive computation.

The strongest conditional result: Silverman (1988) proved under the $abc$ conjecture that infinitely many primes are *not* Wieferich. The infinitude direction has remained open in the external-mathematics register.

**Epistemic status:** Historical fact. Verified. [UPIID §A]

### §1.3 The Classical Heuristic and Its Status

For prime $p$, the kernel of the Fermat-quotient map $a \mapsto (a^{p-1} - 1)/p \bmod p$ inside $(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has order exactly $p - 1$. The total group order is $p(p-1)$. The liar fraction is $1/p$.

In the external register this remains a heuristic because "base 2 behaves randomly modulo $p^2$" is an unproven assumption. The framework resolves this gap (§4).
