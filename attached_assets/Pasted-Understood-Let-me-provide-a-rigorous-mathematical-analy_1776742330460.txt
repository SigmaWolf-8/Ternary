## The Gabor Atom: Rigorous Analysis

$$f(t) = e^{-\gamma (t-t_0)^2} \cdot e^{i\omega t}, \quad \gamma > 0, \ \omega \in \mathbb{R}, \ t_0 \in \mathbb{R}$$

### 1. Projections onto Coordinate Planes

Write $f(t) = x(t) + i y(t)$:

$$x(t) = \text{Re}[f(t)] = e^{-\gamma (t-t_0)^2} \cos(\omega t)$$

$$y(t) = \text{Im}[f(t)] = e^{-\gamma (t-t_0)^2} \sin(\omega t)$$

**Projection 1 (Re-t plane):** The curve $(t, x(t))$ is a damped cosine.

**Projection 2 (Im-t plane):** The curve $(t, y(t))$ is a damped sine.

**Projection 3 (Re-Im plane):** The parametric curve $(x(t), y(t))$ satisfies:
$$x(t)^2 + y(t)^2 = e^{-2\gamma (t-t_0)^2}$$
which is a spiral inward toward $(0,0)$ as $|t| \to \infty$. The polar coordinates are:
$$r(t) = e^{-\gamma (t-t_0)^2}, \quad \theta(t) = \omega t$$

### 2. Connection to the Pythagorean Identity

For any $t$, the identity $x(t)^2 + y(t)^2 = r(t)^2$ holds. This is a direct consequence of $e^{i\omega t} = \cos\omega t + i\sin\omega t$ and $|e^{i\omega t}| = 1$.

The invariant radius $r(t)$ decays, but the **instantaneous Pythagorean relation** $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ is a continuous manifestation of the geometric relation from **IMG_1121** ($1^2 + (\sqrt{2})^2 = (\sqrt{3})^2$). Here the "hypotenuse" $r(t)$ is not constant but follows a Gaussian decay.
