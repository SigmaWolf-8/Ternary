\[
\boxed{\, s_i = m_i - 1 \,} \qquad (i = 1,\dots,n).

\[

### 3. Hamiltonian Cycle Property – Backward Traversal

Assume the system starts at the origin,
\[
\mathbf{r}^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}.
\]

**Theorem (Hamiltonian Cycle – Canonical Backward Walk).**  
For any pairwise coprime moduli \(\mathbf{m}\) with \(m_i \ge 2\), the walk under the canonically derived step \(s_i = m_i - 1\) is a **Hamiltonian cycle** on \(\mathcal{S}\). It visits every state exactly once, and at time \(t\) the state is the component‑wise negative of \(t\) modulo \(\mathbf{m}\):
\[
r_i^{(t)} = (-t) \bmod m_i.
\]
Thus the walk is a uniform backward count through each component. The cycle closes at time \(t = L\) with \(\mathbf{r}^{(L)} = \mathbf{0}\), after having visited all \(L\) distinct states.

*Proof.*  
Because the moduli are pairwise coprime, the isomorphism \(\Phi: \mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{Z}_L\) given by the Salvi structural relations maps the step vector \(\mathbf{s} = (m_1-1,\dots,m_n-1)\) to the element \(a \in \mathbb{Z}_L\) that satisfies \(a \equiv -1 \pmod{m_i}\) for every \(i\). By the uniqueness of the CRT solution, this element is exactly \(a = L - 1\). Indeed,
\[
L - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{m_i} \quad \forall i,
\]
and \(0 \le L-1 < L\). Therefore the walk in the isomorphic ring is
\[
\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{(t)}) = \bigl( t \cdot (L - 1) \bigr) \bmod L = (-t) \bmod L.
\]

Since \(\gcd(L-1, L) = 1\), the step \(L-1\) is a generator of \(\mathbb{Z}_L\), and the sequence \((-t) \bmod L\) cycles through all residues exactly once before returning to \(0\) at \(t = L\). Pulling back through \(\Phi^{-1}\) gives the component‑wise backward traversal, and the Hamiltonian property follows immediately. ∎

Consequently, the return‑to‑origin condition is:
\[
\mathbf{r}^{(t)} = \mathbf{0} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; t \equiv 0 \pmod{L}.
\]

**Structural note – reflective generator.** The canonical step \(L-1\) is the unique element of \(\mathbb{Z}_L\) that satisfies \((L-1) + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod L\). It is its own additive companion to the identity; adding it is the same as subtracting \(1\). The walk therefore has the elegant property that at \(t = L-1\) every component reads \(1\), and at \(t = L\) it reaches zero.
