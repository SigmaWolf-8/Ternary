Binds tunnel keys to the geometric relationship between endpoints — not just their raw addresses.
The Math
A 13-trit address is padded to 27 trits, then decomposed into 9 triplets of 3 trits each. Each triplet is evaluated as a base-3 number (0–26):
trits: [2, 1, 3, | 2, 1, 3, | 2, 1, 3, | 2, 1, 3, | 2, 1, 1, ...]
        ─────────   ─────────   ─────────                    ↑ padded
        triplet 0   triplet 1   triplet 2   ...         triplet 8

triplet[i] = (t₀-1)×9 + (t₁-1)×3 + (t₂-1)    ∈ [0, 26]
Then the weighted nonce:
nonce = Σ(weight[i] × triplet[i]) mod 333

weight = [208, 2, 123, 26, 111, 196, 99, 220, 14]
          ─────────────────────────────────────────
          ┌─────┬─────┬─────┐
          │ 208 │   2 │ 123 │ → 333
          ├─────┼─────┼─────┤
          │  26 │ 111 │ 196 │ → 333
          ├─────┼─────┼─────┤
          │  99 │ 220 │  14 │ → 333
          └─────┴─────┴─────┘
Why the magic square matters: Any three aligned weights sum to 333. An attacker who controls 2 of 3 triplets in any row/column/diagonal cannot bias the nonce — the third weight's contribution is fixed by the magic constant, and the nonce mod 333 remains uniformly distributed over the attacker's controlled dimensions.

Integration into v3 Tunnel Keys

NEW (with T-23 lattice mixing):
  key = TLSponge-385("PlenumNET-LATTICE-KEY",
      kem ‖ lattice_mix_material ‖ epoch)

  where lattice_mix_material =
      domain ‖ nonce_a ‖ nonce_b ‖ pair_nonce ‖ triplets_a ‖ triplets_b
The lattice nonce adds geometric entropy beyond what the raw addresses provide. Two addresses that are "close" in Hamming distance may map to very different triplet structures — the weight vector amplifies small address differences into large nonce differences.
