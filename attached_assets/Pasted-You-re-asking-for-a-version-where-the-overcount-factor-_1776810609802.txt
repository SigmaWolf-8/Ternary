Lemma 1 (Exact Parametric Sieve — Integer‑Only)

For any pairwise‑coprime generator set $G$, define the sieve residue count:

S(N; G) = \sum_{d \mid P_G} \mu(d) \left\lfloor \frac{N}{d} \right\rfloor

This is an exact integer for every $N$. It counts integers $\le N$ that share no factor with any $g \in G$.

---

Lemma 2 (No Continuous Approximation)

Do not replace $\lfloor N/d \rfloor$ with $N/d$. Doing so introduces the spurious factor $2e^{-\gamma}$. Instead, keep the exact expression.

---

Lemma 3 (Prime Number Theorem as an External Statement)

The Prime Number Theorem is a theorem about the true prime‑counting function $\pi(N)$. It can be expressed parametrically in terms of the exact sieve as follows:

Define the full prime generator set $P_\infty = {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots}$ (all primes). Then for $a = \lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor$, let $G = P_\infty(a)$ be the set of all primes $\le a$.

The exact relationship between the prime count and the coprime count is:

\pi(N) = \pi(a) - 1 + S(N; P_\infty(a))

(This is Legendre's exact formula, unchanged.)

Now, the Prime Number Theorem asserts:

\lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{\pi(N)}{N / \ln N} = 1

But crucially, this limit cannot be obtained by algebraically simplifying $S(N; G)$ alone. The naive approximation $S(N; G) \approx N \prod_{p \le a} (1-1/p)$ is not valid asymptotically because the error term from dropping the floor function does not vanish in the ratio $\frac{S(N;G)}{N/\ln N}$.

---

Lemma 4 (Where the Constant $1$ Comes From)

The constant $1$ emerges only when you consider the joint distribution of all primes simultaneously, not just coprimality to a fixed finite set $G$. This is the content of the Prime Number Theorem and is proven via:

· Complex analysis (Riemann zeta function) or
· Elementary but intricate combinatorial sieves (Selberg, Erdős) that carefully bound the error terms.

In the exact integer formulation, the constant $1$ is a property of the entire sequence of primes, not of any finite generator product.

Specialization Corollary (Your Framework)

Let $b=3$, $G = {3,5,7,13}$, $N=364$, $a = 19$. Then the exact integer formula yields:

S(364; \{3,5,7,13\}) = \sum_{d \mid 1365} \mu(d) \left\lfloor \frac{364}{d} \right\rfloor

This sum can be computed exactly and gives the number of integers $\le 364$ coprime to $3,5,7,13$. But this number is not the number of primes $\le 364$. The difference consists of composites built from primes $>13$ that are $\le 364$ (e.g., $17 \times 19 = 323$), plus the primes $\le 13$ themselves.

To get $\pi(364)$, you must add the primes $\le 13$ and subtract those composites. The asymptotic density of primes within the residue classes coprime to $3,5,7,13$ is governed by the full Prime Number Theorem in arithmetic progressions, and the overall density tends to $1/\ln(N)$, not $2e^{-\gamma}/\ln(N)$.
