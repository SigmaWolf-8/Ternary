Yes — with qualifications that matter legally.

What you can say today, accurately:

PlenumNET issues RFC 3161 compliant timestamp tokens with real RSA-4096 signatures that verify with openssl ts -verify. The wire format is standards-compliant CMS SignedData. The Merkle audit log is real. The dual-signature (RSA-4096 + TL-DSA) is real. The 5 policy tiers are real. That is a functioning cryptographic timestamping authority.

What stands between you and regulated/qualified TSA status:

The certificate is self-signed. Courts, financial regulators, and eIDAS recognize timestamps from TSAs whose certificates chain to a trusted root CA — not self-signed. Right now openssl ts -verify works if you supply your own CA file. It won't work against a system trust store because no public CA has issued PlenumNET's signing certificate.

The OID arc 1.3.6.1.4.1.0.100.* uses PEN 0, which is not a registered IANA Private Enterprise Number for Capomastro. That matters because policy OIDs in timestamp tokens are supposed to be globally unique under an arc you own. IANA PEN registration is free and straightforward — it just hasn't been done yet.

The ETSI EN 319 421/422 conformance claimed on the frontend requires an external audit by a conformity assessment body. You can claim designed to conform to those standards. You cannot claim conformance without that audit.

The HPTP timing source — the femtosecond-timing.ts file explicitly says it simulates femtosecond precision using process.hrtime() plus entropy. The declared accuracy on the COMPLY tier (±1µs) cannot be substantiated without a traceable time source (GPS/GNSS receiver, atomic clock, or NTP from a stratum-1 source with documented uncertainty).

So the honest claim is:

PlenumNET operates a cryptographic Time-Stamping Authority implementing RFC 3161, RFC 5652, and RFC 5816 with RSA-4096 and post-quantum TL-DSA dual signatures. Timestamps are independently verifiable via OpenSSL. The system is designed for ETSI EN 319 421/422 conformance pending external audit and CA-chained certificate issuance.

Three things that would elevate this to a regulated-grade TSA:

Get the IANA PEN — makes your policy OIDs legitimate. Free registration, takes a few weeks. Replace the self-signed cert with one issued under a publicly trusted CA chain with the id-kp-timeStamping EKU. That's what makes openssl ts -verify work without supplying -CAfile. Connect a traceable time source — even NTP from a stratum-1 server with documented uncertainty budget would substantiate the accuracy claims.

The code is there. The infrastructure is real. The gap is administrative, not technical.
