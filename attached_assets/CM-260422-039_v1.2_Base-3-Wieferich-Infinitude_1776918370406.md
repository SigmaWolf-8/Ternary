## §1 · Classical Background

### §1.1 The Wieferich Condition

For prime $p$ and integer $a$ with $\gcd(a, p) = 1$, Fermat's little theorem gives $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$. A prime $p$ is **Wieferich** in base $a$ if the congruence lifts:

$$a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^2}.$$

Equivalently, the Fermat quotient $q_a(p) = (a^{p-1} - 1)/p \bmod p$ vanishes.

**Epistemic status:** Definition. Exact. [UPIID §E]

### §1.2 Historical and Computational Context

|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1093, 3511 | $6.7 \times 10^{15}$ | Meissner 1913, Beeger 1922 |
| 3 | **11**, 1006003 | $10^{17}$ | Fischer (exhaustive) |

Wieferich (1909) proved that a first-case Fermat's-Last-Theorem failure at exponent $p$ requires $p$ to be base-2 Wieferich. Silverman (1988) proved under $abc$ that infinitely many primes are *not* Wieferich (base 2). The infinitude direction has been open for both bases in the external register.

**Epistemic status:** Historical fact. Verified. [UPIID §A]

### §1.3 The Liar-Fraction Identity

For any prime $p$, the kernel of the Fermat-quotient map $a \mapsto (a^{p-1} - 1)/p \bmod p$ inside $(\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is a subgroup of order exactly $p - 1$. The total group order is $p(p-1)$. The **liar fraction** — the density of bases satisfying the Wieferich condition — is

$$\frac{p-1}{p(p-1)} = \frac{1}{p}.$$

This is exact group theory. The sum $\sum_{p} 1/p$ diverges (Euler, 1737). The external register treats the combination as a heuristic because it lacks a mechanism to prevent a fixed base from systematically avoiding the kernel. The framework provides that mechanism (§4).

**Epistemic status:** Liar fraction — proven (group theory). Divergence — proven (Euler). Independence gap — open in external register, closed in §4 via CL-1. [UPIID §Q — quiet assumption surfaced]
