## SALVI METHOD REFERENCE

Each method describes the algorithm for converting Julian Day Number to the target system. Reference: Dershowitz & Reingold, *Calendrical Calculations* (4th ed.) for formally documented systems; primary sources for cultural/ecological systems.

| Salvi Method | Systems | Algorithm |
|-------------|---------|-----------|
| **Direct anchor** | `gregorian` | Base reference. JDN ↔ Gregorian is the foundation conversion. All other systems ultimately route through this or directly from JDN. |
| **Universal intermediary** | `julian-day` | Identity function. JDN is the intermediary itself — continuous integer day count since 4713 BCE January 1 (Julian proleptic). |
| **Epoch offset** | `assyrian`, `minguo` | JDN → Gregorian year ± fixed constant. Assyrian = Gregorian + 4750. Minguo = Gregorian − 1911. Computationally trivial but legally significant (Assyrian institutional publications, Taiwan government filings). |
| **Parinibbāna / era offset** | `buddhist`, `japanese` | JDN → Gregorian year + era constant. BE = Gregorian + 543. Kōki = Gregorian + 660. Japanese also carries current era name (Reiwa since 2019). |
| **Foundation offset** | `korean` | JDN → Gregorian year + 2333. Dangun founding myth of Gojoseon. Cultural reference on Korean government documents. |
| **Offset calc (Sept epoch)** | `byzantine` | JDN → Julian year + 5509. Year begins September 1 (not January 1). Months follow Julian calendar. Used in Eastern Orthodox liturgical computations. |
| **Metonic cycle** | `hebrew`, `vikram-samvat`, `nepal-sambat` | JDN → lunisolar with 19-year intercalation. Hebrew adds leap month Adar II seven times per 19-year cycle. Vikram Samvat uses sidereal year with adhika māsa (extra month) insertion. Nepal Sambat follows distinct Newar intercalation rules from 879 CE epoch. |
| **60-year cycle** | `chinese`, `tibetan` | JDN → lunisolar with sexagenary stem-branch mapping. 10 Heavenly Stems × 12 Earthly Branches = 60-year cycle. Chinese uses Beijing meridian; Tibetan Rabjung starts 1027 CE with distinct intercalation. |
| **30-year cycle** | `hijri` | JDN → pure lunar. 12 months alternating 29/30 days. Leap day added 11 times per 30-year cycle (years 2, 5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 24, 26, 29). Year drifts ~11 days vs solar annually. |
| **Jalali algorithm** | `solar-hijri` | JDN → solar with astronomical observation of vernal equinox at Tehran meridian (52.5°E). More accurate than Gregorian leap rules over multi-century periods. Grand cycle of 2820 years. |
| **Sothic cycle** | `egyptian` | JDN → fixed 365-day year with no leap day. Drifts 1 day per 4 years against the tropical year. Full Sothic cycle (alignment with heliacal rising of Sirius) = 1,461 Egyptian years. Three seasons of four months each (Akhet, Peret, Shemu). |
| **Saka era calc** | `indian-saka` | JDN → solar with tropical zodiac month boundaries. Month 1 (Chaitra) begins at vernal equinox (~March 22). Chaitra has 30 days in leap years, 31 in normal — inverse of Gregorian convention. Official Indian national calendar since 1957. |

## INCLUSION CRITERIA

For any calendar system to enter this registry, it must satisfy **at least one** of:

1. **Official civil calendar** of a UN member state
2. **Co-official or constitutionally referenced** in a sovereign nation
3. **Religious calendar** governing institutional life of a community exceeding 1 million adherents
4. **Treaty-recognized** Indigenous system with formal legal standing
5. **Computationally distinct** date-reckoning method with documented cultural depth exceeding 500 years

**Exclusion rules:**

- Trivial offsets of existing entries (no distinct reckoning method)
- Formatting conventions on existing calendars (display modes, not reckoning systems)
- Systems deprecated by their own governments or institutions
- Holidays and observances are events ON calendars, not calendar systems
- Operational schedules (retention timers, cert rotation) are infrastructure, not calendars

**Process:** Addition requires two independent scholarly or governmental sources confirming computational distinctness and active use. Deprecation requires official government or institutional cessation.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

1. **Calendar enrichment NEVER blocks token issuance.** Best-effort, same as TL-DSA co-signature.
2. **Extension is NON-CRITICAL** (`critical: FALSE` in ASN.1). Standard verifiers skip it.
3. **Request-level calendars are ADDITIVE.** Merge with policy defaults, never replace.
4. **No calendars resolved = no extension.** Clean token, zero overhead.
5. **Calendar data is derived, not authoritative.** UTC genTime from HPTP is the legal timestamp.
6. **Schema versioned** (JSON payload `"v": 1`). Bump on format changes.
7. **Julian Day Number is the bridge.** Included in every CalendarContext for independent verification.
8. **Source attribution.** Every CalendarContext records policy vs request origin.
9. **Invalid system names silently dropped.** Warning logged, token not blocked.
10. **No duplicate keys.** Each CALENDAR_SYSTEMS entry maps to a computationally distinct system.
11. **"Vedic" is not a calendar.** Kali Yuga is. The distinction is non-negotiable.
12. **Holidays are not calendar systems.** Events on calendars are not date-reckoning methods.
13. **Operational schedules are not calendar systems.** Retention timers and cert rotation are infrastructure.
14. **Inclusion criteria are formal.** No system enters without meeting at least one criterion plus two independent sources.
