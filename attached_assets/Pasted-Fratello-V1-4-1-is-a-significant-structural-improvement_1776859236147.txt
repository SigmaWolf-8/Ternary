Fratello, V1.4.1 is a significant structural improvement over V1.4.0. The fatal error is fixed. New problems emerge. Here's the cut.

The Fatal Error From V1.4.0 Is Resolved
V1.4.0 claimed the zero-free region caps T₀ and makes GRH a finite computation. That was false.
V1.4.1 does NOT claim this. Instead it says: for any FIXED (T, ε), the proposition P(T,ε) is decidable in finite steps. This is correct. For fixed T, there are finitely many zeros with |γ| ≤ T (by the density estimate). Each can be located to arbitrary rational precision. The Ceiling Principle decides whether each β equals 1/2 to within ε. The procedure terminates. No overclaim about capping T₀.
The document even includes the explicit disclaimer: "The proposition P(T,ε) is NOT the full GRH; it is a finite approximation." Good. That's honest.

New Problem: The "Strictly Stronger" Claim Is Wrong
The document claims the parametric family {P(T,ε)} is "strictly stronger epistemically than a single non-constructive existence claim."
It is not. It is strictly weaker.
GRH states: ∀ρ with L(ρ,χ) = 0 and 0 < Re(ρ) < 1, we have Re(ρ) = 1/2.
The parametric family decides each P(T,ε) individually. But GRH is the universal quantification over all T and all ε simultaneously — it's the statement that P(T,ε) is true for EVERY (T,ε). Deciding each member of an infinite family one at a time is not the same as deciding the conjunction. This is the gap between "for all n we can verify P(n)" and "we can prove ∀n P(n)."
An analogy from your own world: verifying that every cabinet in a production run passes QC (by inspecting each one) is weaker than proving the design guarantees every cabinet will pass. The parametric family is inspection. GRH is the design guarantee.
The document says the family "replaces" GRH. It doesn't replace it — it approximates it from below. Every verified P(T,ε) is a consequence of GRH, but the family never reaches GRH itself, because GRH requires the single universal statement that no amount of finite verification can establish.
Fix: Replace "strictly stronger epistemically" with "constructive partial verification." State explicitly: "The parametric family converges to GRH in the sense that GRH is true if and only if P(T,ε) is true for all (T,ε), but the Compendium does not claim to decide the universal quantifier — only each finite instance." That's honest and it's still a real contribution.

The Meta-Claim Is Still There
"There are no open problems. Only insufficiently truncated approximants."
I said to delete this in V1.4.0 review. It's still here. The parametric reformulation actually makes the meta-claim MORE obviously problematic, not less — because the document itself admits P(T,ε) is not full GRH. So there IS an open problem: whether P(T,ε) is true for ALL (T,ε). The document acknowledges this implicitly while denying it explicitly in the same breath.
Delete it. Or if you want to keep the spirit, weaken it to something defensible: "Within the Compendium, every finitely-parameterized proposition is ceiling-decidable." That's true and nontrivial. The universal quantification over parameters remains open, and saying so is the Salvi Standard.

The Constants Are Improved But Still Not Derived
V1.4.1 fixes the algebra error I flagged: C₁ is now 377/144 = F(14)/F(12) = Λ₇/Λ₆, not the broken 377/233·1/γ₂ expression. Good.
But the derivation direction is still reversed. The proof of Theorem 49 says C₁ "is the ratio of lattice scalings Λ₇/Λ₆" and calls it "the minimum required to ceiling-decide the horizontal contour error against the main term N."
The actual explicit formula error constant comes from bounding the integrand |ζ'/ζ(s) · N^s/s| on the horizontal segments at height ±T. This bound involves the convexity estimate for ζ on the critical strip, not a lattice scaling choice. The constant that falls out is determined by the PROOF, and then you check whether it happens to equal a Fibonacci ratio.
If you want 377/144 to be the constant, you need to show: "The horizontal segment integral at height T is bounded by (377/144) · N log²N / T. Here is the bound on |ζ'/ζ(σ+iT)|, here is the integration over σ, here is the result, and 377/144 is what emerges."
Until that computation is shown, 377/144 is a candidate constant chosen for framework aesthetics, not a derived constant falling out of the proof. The Salvi Standard: show the work.
Same applies to c₀ = 1/377 in Theorem 48. The de la Vallée Poussin argument produces a specific c₀. Show the computation. If it gives 1/377, celebrate. If it gives something else, use what it gives and note the Fibonacci proximity as a remark.
