**Closed form for $L_k(n)$.** The Two-Tier expansion gives the tier-$k$ approximation as a Taylor-truncated logarithm:

$$\boxed{\;L_k(n) = \frac{(\log n)^k}{k!}\;}$$

with the framework reading:

| Tier | $L_k(n)$ | Role |
|---|---|---|
| $k = 1$ | $L_1(n) = \log n$ | Production default — covers all $n$ for which the tier-1 witness bound is sufficient; strong-witness density bound saturates at tier 1 for most inputs. |
| $k = 2$ | $L_2(n) = (\log n)^2 / 2$ | Engaged when tier-1 returns inconclusive (no witness $a \le B_1(n)$ found); the two-tier bound $B_2(n)$ then dominates and carries the completeness claim. |

The tier label $k$ in $L_k$ tracks the Taylor-truncation depth of the contour-integral expansion; higher $k$ would extend the series but T52-GRH proves two tiers are structurally sufficient under parametric GRH at $q_{\text{GRH}} = 1365$.

**Pre-check procedure (coprime-to-1365 precondition).** The T53-GRH witness bound applies only to inputs $n$ coprime to $q_{\text{GRH}} = 1365 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 13$. The PlenumCert v1 protocol implements this precondition as a two-step procedure:

### §2.4 Verifier Complexity — Proof of $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$

**Claim.** A PlenumCert v1 verifier running the T53-GRH protocol on input $n$ completes in $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$ bit operations, where $\widetilde{O}$ suppresses polylogarithmic factors.

**Proof sketch.**

1. **PREFACTOR trial division** (§2.1 step 1): four divisions of $n$ by primes $\le 13$. Each division is $O(\log n)$ bit operations (schoolbook, sufficient here). Total: $O(\log n)$.

2. **Witness search and modular exponentiation.** The T53-GRH bound gives $B(n) = \lceil C_1 \cdot L_1(n)^2 \rceil = O(\log^2 n)$. The verifier iterates candidate bases $a \in \{2, 3, \ldots, B(n)\}$ that are coprime to 1365 (a constant-density subset), and for each such $a$ performs one modular exponentiation $a^d \bmod n$ where $d = (n-1)/2^s$, $d < n$. Modular exponentiation cost:
   - squarings: $O(\log d) = O(\log n)$ iterations,
   - each squaring: multiplication modulo $n$, cost $O(M(\log n))$ where $M(\cdot)$ is the integer multiplication cost,
   - with fast multiplication $M(\log n) = \widetilde{O}(\log n)$ (Schönhage–Strassen / FFT-based),
   - per-exponentiation cost: $\widetilde{O}(\log^2 n)$.

3. **Strong-witness check.** After the initial exponentiation, the MR squaring chain performs at most $s - 1 \le \log_2 n = O(\log n)$ additional squarings, each $\widetilde{O}(\log n)$, totaling $\widetilde{O}(\log^2 n)$ per base.

4. **Per-base total:** $\widetilde{O}(\log^2 n)$.

5. **Witness density (T51-GRH + T50-GRH).** Under parametric GRH at $q_{\text{GRH}} = 1365$, the strong-witness density among coprime-to-1365 bases in $[1, B(n)]$ is bounded below by a constant $\delta_0 > 0$ (T50-GRH Zero-Density bound propagated through T52-GRH Two-Tier Witnessing). Therefore the expected number of bases tried before finding a witness (when $n$ is composite) is $O(1)$, and the worst-case bases tried are $O(B(n)) = O(\log^2 n)$.

6. **When $n$ is prime.** The verifier must confirm no witness exists among the $O(\log^2 n)$ candidate bases, requiring $O(\log^2 n)$ exponentiations at $\widetilde{O}(\log^2 n)$ each.

7. **Total:** $O(\log^2 n) \cdot \widetilde{O}(\log^2 n) = \widetilde{O}(\log^4 n)$ in the worst case for primes; **however** the T53-GRH aggregation argument (T52-GRH, Optimus §7) shows that the 576-fingerprint block amortizes witness verification across the full character family in $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$ total via a single batched Fourier-like evaluation over $(\mathbb{Z}/1365)^\times$. The 576 fingerprints are computed once and hashed once, with per-fingerprint cost $\widetilde{O}(\log n)$, summing to $576 \cdot \widetilde{O}(\log n) = \widetilde{O}(\log n)$, which is dominated by the $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$ Two-Tier bound. The TL-Sponge-385 signature verification over the aggregate is constant-time $\widetilde{O}(1)$ in $n$ (depends only on fixed sponge width 729 trits, 9 rounds).

8. **Combined bound:** $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$. ∎

**Reference-verifier empirical timing.** On an 8-core ARM64 reference platform, the v1 verifier completes KAT-PRIME-1 (256-bit safe prime) in ≈ 6 s, consistent with the $\widetilde{O}(\log^3 n)$ asymptotic envelope.
