Here's what was learned and what was kept:
What v1 taught us (then buried)
v1 put a sponge call at every walk step. That's 11 sponge calls for signing, 324 for verification. The per-step commitment was 48 bytes × h, making signatures 482 bytes and variable-length. The only thing v1 got right was the 26-port simulation measuring per-port latency independently — not re-running the full sequential path.
Kept from v1: The parallel verify path does NOT call verify() internally. Three clean phases: sponge 1 → parallel port checks → sponge 2. Zero redundancy. This is why v1's 26-port sim hit 20µs while v2's hit 54µs — v2 was doing double work.
What v2 taught us (then merged)
v2 replaced per-step sponge calls with GF(3) trit arithmetic at 313–653 picoseconds. Step tokens use Plenum Square weighted triplet evaluation mod 333. Walk tokens accumulate with position-weighting for order sensitivity. Walk parity uses the same row/column/diagonal ECC structure as the wire protocol.
Kept from v2: All the GF(3) arithmetic. The 71-byte fixed signature. The 2-sponge-call architecture.

Walk engine (zero sponge calls):
  execute_walk()   — full walk from addr→dest, returns (token, parity, length)

Sponge layer (2 calls per operation):
  compute_binding() — single TLSponge-385 over all geometric outputs
  derive_dest()     — message → destination vertex

Public API:
  keygen()          — 2 sponge calls
  sign()            — 2 sponge calls + ~650ps geometry
